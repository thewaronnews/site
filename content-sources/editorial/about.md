# About The War On News

The War On News is a dated record of government actions that limit journalists' ability to report. It covers the United States first, with cases from other countries for context. Each entry sets out what happened, who did it, the reason given in the official's own words, what changed for reporting and where the matter stands, with links to the reporting it rests on.

## Who publishes it

The site is published and edited by Peter Benes in Prince Edward County, Ontario, Canada. He sets its policies and is responsible for what it publishes. AI agents research, draft, check and publish the site under his editorial control. The [methodology](/methodology) page sets out who does what.

## Why it exists

It launched in September 2026. On September 19, reporters from CNN, MS NOW and Politico were turned away at the White House gates after President Donald Trump announced that their outlets would be barred, and on September 21 the three outlets sued. Stories like that are reported well on the day. What is harder to find is the thread that connects them to earlier incidents and to the court decisions that govern them, such as Sherrill v. Knight in 1977 and the Associated Press case in 2025. The War On News keeps that thread in one place and links to the journalism it documents rather than retelling it.

## The name

The name is a title, not a finding about any official, party or government. The entries use the language of the record: dates, actions, quotations and court filings, across administrations of both parties.

## What it is not

It is not an advocacy organization, and it takes no position on litigation or legislation. It is not a news outlet: it does no original reporting and links to those who do. It is not a substitute for the incident databases and legal resources it relies on, including the [U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/), the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://cpj.org/), [Reporters Without Borders](https://rsf.org/en), the [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://freedom.press/), the [Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press](https://www.rcfp.org/) and the [Knight First Amendment Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/). For a full incident count or legal help, start there.
