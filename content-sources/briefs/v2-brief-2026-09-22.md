# The War On News, v2 brief (architect, 2026-09-22)

Peter reviewed the v1 launch and redirected the concept. This brief supersedes the proposal and the v1 spec wherever they conflict. Everything below is binding.

## Concept
"How governments have limited journalists, 1900 to today." A sourced record with three ways in:
1. By place: country, grouped by continent. The United States is the catalyst and focal point (the September 2026 White House ban of CNN, MS NOW and Politico) and is presented as one country among many. The publisher is Canadian; nothing on the site may read as if written by or for an American. No "domestic/foreign" framing anywhere. Always name the country and jurisdiction.
2. By tactic (pattern). Fixed taxonomy, each with its own page: access_ban (barring reporters or outlets from briefings, buildings, pools, travel), credential_control (passes, accreditation, licensing of journalists), outlet_licensing (licensing, registration or closure of outlets, broadcast licences), prior_restraint (censorship boards, injunctions, pre-publication review), secrets_and_espionage_laws (Espionage Act, Official Secrets, state-secrets prosecutions of journalists and sources), insult_and_defamation_laws (criminal defamation, lèse-majesté, "fake news" laws), surveillance_and_subpoenas (seizure of records, subpoenas, spyware), funding_and_ownership_pressure (defunding public media, forced sales, advertising pressure, regulatory pressure on owners), expulsion_and_visa_denial (foreign correspondents), shutdowns_and_blocking (internet shutdowns, blocking of sites, jamming), detention_and_violence (arrests, imprisonment, assault, killing, where a government actor is involved), lawsuits_against_press (government or officials suing outlets), disinformation_labeling (official "fake news" designations, "foreign agent" labels). An incident may carry more than one tactic; one is primary.
3. By time: an era timeline from 1900 (general, anchor incidents per decade and region), granular from 2020 (every well-sourced incident). Eras are computed from dates; no separate table.

Comparison view ("Who did what, when"): a matrix of tactic × country with the dated incidents, the head of government or responsible official at the time, the issue of the day (the story the restriction responded to, when the sources say), and the outcome (reversed, upheld by a court, sustained, ongoing, unknown). Every cell links to incidents and every incident to journalistic-quality sources.

## Data model changes (migration 0004)
- incidents: drop the domestic/foreign meaning of `level`; keep level as the tier of government (national, state_or_province, municipal, supranational). Add `continent` (derived from country), `tactic_primary`, `tactics` (join table incident_tactics), `leader_slug` (actor who headed the government at the time, may differ from the acting official), `issue_of_the_day` (≤60 words, sourced), `outcome` (enum: reversed, upheld, sustained, ongoing, unknown) with `outcome_on` and `outcome_note`, `era` (computed: 1900s … 2020s), `granularity` (anchor | granular).
- tactics table: slug, name, definition (≤80 words), first_recorded_on, notes.
- countries table: iso2, name, continent, region (UN M49), press_freedom_rank_latest (RSF, with year and source), notes.
- Recent coverage: table `coverage_items` (url, title, publisher, published_at, summary ≤40 words from the feed, jev_in_scope score, tactic guess, country guess, incident_id nullable, state: shown | hidden). Fetched hourly by the Worker's cron from a curated feed list plus Google News RSS queries; Jev scores in the Worker (HTTP call); dedupe by canonical URL and title similarity; shown when score ≥ threshold; no editorial text; every item links out with the site's standard external-link markup. The News Desk notes table, digest and desk token scope are retired (keep the tables, stop the pipeline; remove the routes and nav).
- Drop from public copy and nav: "News Desk". Add: /coverage (recent coverage), /countries, /countries/<iso2>, /continents/<name>, /tactics, /tactics/<slug>, /compare (matrix), /eras/<decade>, /leaders/<slug> (an actor view for heads of government).

## Search and filter
- Full-text search across incidents, actors, outlets, cases, tactics, glossary, coverage (FTS5 exists in migration 0003; extend).
- Facets on /incidents and /search: country, continent, tactic, year range (from/to), level, actor or leader, outlet, outcome, source kind, has_court_case. All as query parameters; every filtered view has a canonical URL, a title, and .md and .json twins; sort by date, country, tactic. Table view and card view. Results count and "download this view as CSV/JSON".
- The MCP tools take the same facets (search_incidents gains country, continent, tactic, leader, outcome, from, to).

## Copy rules (replace v1 pages)
- Footer: "The War On News is published by [Peter Benes](/about)." then "© 2026 Peter Benes. Text and data are licensed CC BY 4.0 unless noted; quotations remain the property of their sources." Links: Terms of use · Privacy · Corrections · Sources and standards · Contact · Data · MCP · Feeds.
- About: why the subject matters (journalism as foundational to democracy; the United States as the focal case; the value of seeing which other countries use the same tactics, their approaches and outcomes), what the site is and is not, the sources it relies on, who publishes it, contact. One line on AI: "The development of this site and its content were assisted with AI." Nothing about agents, pipelines, gates, classifiers or how the work is performed.
- "Methodology" becomes "Sources and standards": source hierarchy (court records and primary documents; reporting by established news organisations; reports by press-freedom organisations), the quotation rule, dating and precision, corrections and supersession, link checking and archiving (one sentence), and the licence. No process narration.
- Terms of use (Ontario, Canada governing law; informational, not legal advice; accuracy not warranted; quotations and links belong to their sources; CC BY 4.0 grant for the site's own text and data with attribution "The War On News, thewaronnews.com"; no scraping restrictions beyond rate limits; notice-and-takedown for copyright or accuracy complaints via corrections@; limitation of liability; changes). Privacy (Cloudflare edge analytics only, no cookies, no accounts; correspondence handling; MCP and form submissions stored for moderation). Mark both "Draft for legal review" in the worklog, not on the page.
- Keep the documentary register. Sources must be journalistic-quality or primary; blogs, advocacy statements and social posts only as the subject of an incident, not as its evidence.
- Reader assumption (Peter, 2026-09-22): the reader may be anywhere in the world, is intelligent but ignorant of the subject, and an American reader may know little about their own government and have limited access to news. So: never treat a law, court, office or institution as an obvious reference. On first mention in any page, give the one-clause gloss: "the White House Correspondents' Association, the organisation of reporters who cover the president", "the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), the United States regulator of broadcasting", "the Espionage Act, a 1917 United States law against disclosing defence information", "hard pass, the standing credential that lets a reporter enter the White House press area". Glossary terms are linked on first mention. Country is always named; "the president" is "the president of the United States" on first mention.
- Link-state badges ("Live", "Blocks automatic checks") are removed from public pages. They mean nothing to a reader. Keep link states in the data and in the .json twin; on the page, show only the source and, when the original no longer resolves, "archived copy" next to it.
